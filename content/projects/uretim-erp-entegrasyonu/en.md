In a plant, the most valuable data is generated by the machine itself: how much was produced, how long it stopped, how fast it ran, how much energy it used. In this project we put live, in every plant, an architecture where that data flows **automatically and without gaps from the machine to the ERP**.

## Why?

Machine data stayed in automation systems and was keyed into the ERP manually and late. This caused three problems:

- **Gaps between the shop floor and the ERP** in production, inventory and cost reports
- Downtime and efficiency losses **noticed too late**
- Energy consumption that **could not be tracked** by product, line or plant

## Architecture: data flow from machine to ERP

We designed a five-layer architecture where each layer has a clear responsibility:

1. **Field (machines and PLCs):** machine counters, process parameters and energy meters are the data sources.
2. **Communication (Modbus):** standard industrial Modbus communication between the automation software and field devices.
3. **Collection (SAP PCO):** SAP Plant Connectivity reads the tags arriving over Modbus, normalises them and passes them on as events.
4. **Business logic (SAP MII):** Manufacturing Integration and Intelligence turns raw machine data into business events: production confirmations, downtime records, consumption and energy usage.
5. **ERP (SAP S/4HANA):** confirmations, goods movements, downtime and energy data are posted directly to production orders and work centers.

> Data is created once on the shop floor and never keyed in again.

## Scope: what is integrated?

- **Machine counters:** quantities and lengths, with automatic confirmation per production order
- **Machine parameters:** speed, temperature, pressure and other process values that affect quality and efficiency
- **Downtime:** duration and reason for planned and unplanned stops
- **Energy data:** electricity and other energy consumption tracked by machine, line and plant
- **Efficiency (OEE):** every indicator that affects efficiency, across availability, performance and quality

## One standard across all plants

The project did not stop at a single pilot line; it was designed to run **live in every plant**. To achieve this:

- **Shared data dictionary and tag standard:** the same data has the same name and unit in every plant.
- **Master data mapping:** every machine on the floor is mapped to its work center and resource in S/4HANA.
- **Template approach:** the PCO and MII setup built for one plant was rolled out to the others as a template.

## Reliability and security

- **Resilience:** if a connection drops, no data is lost; it is buffered in the middle layer and forwarded in order once the link is back.
- **Time synchronisation:** the shop floor and the ERP share one time reference, so events land on the right shift and order.
- **Monitoring and alerts:** communication errors and inconsistent data are monitored centrally and reported to the right team immediately.
- **OT / IT separation:** production networks are segmented from the corporate network; ERP communication happens only through defined, secure gateways.

## Outcome

- Production confirmations and consumption reach the ERP **without manual entry**.
- Inventory, production and cost data appear in the ERP **at the same time as on the floor**, not at end of day.
- Downtime and efficiency losses are **spotted earlier**, and root-cause analysis is driven by data.
- Energy use is tracked by product and line, making **energy cost per unit** visible.
- The platform provides a **solid data foundation** for MES, predictive maintenance and AI-driven analytics.
